\begin{abstract}
Games, films and robot training need 3D environments that are complete,
editable and owned by their makers; world models and one-shot generators
give frames no engine opens, or one fused scene without separate objects
or clear rights. Studios already build them by a standard: scenes
layered in OpenUSD, each scene sitting within its parent scene and each
department's work on it a layer of its own that can be checked and
changed apart from the rest. SCORE builds environments by this
convention. The creator decides from the top down: he locks an
environment concept (EC), its style and its physics, which holds the
scenes and their layering, and then a concept for each scene, in order
of parent scene and child scene. The framework makes every layer below:
it reads each locked concept into a scene inventory and a plan, and
builds the scene's layers with open vision foundation models, procedural
tools and coding agents, each layer checked before the next is made.
Every model allows commercial use of its output, so the creator stays
the author. We describe the framework with its first environment, 26
scenes derived from the game 2099, as the worked example, and separate
what its runs prove, what is built but has not run, and what is missing:
the first run brought every scene through its completion gate, but its
video was not accepted, and the remake from new designs has not run.
Code and paper are available at
https://github.com/Babon-Innovations-b-v/score (MIT license).
\end{abstract}
