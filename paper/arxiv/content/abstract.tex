\begin{abstract}
Games, films and robot training need 3D environments that are complete,
editable and owned by their makers; world models and one-shot generators
give frames no engine opens, or one fused scene without separate objects
or clear rights. Studios build environments in stages, from concept art
to review. SCORE keeps these stages and automates them with open vision
foundation models, procedural tools and coding agents, while the creator
designs and reviews. The creator locks an environment concept and picks
each place's concept; the pick is read into a scene inventory and
written into a plan, and only such a design starts paid work, bought as
one batch with one estimate and a stop at its high estimate plus 10
percent. Every stage is a node of one runner: its output is keyed on its
inputs and code, judged by gates that answer pass, fail or unknown, and
accepted only when nothing got worse, so a fault is fixed in its own
stage and only what follows it runs again. Every model allows commercial
use of its output, so the creator stays the author. We describe the
framework with its first environment, 26 places derived from the game
2099, as the worked example, and separate what its runs prove, what is
built but has not run, and what is missing: the first run brought every
place through its completion gate, but its video was not accepted, and
the remake from new designs has not run. Code and paper are available at
https://github.com/Babon-Innovations-b-v/score (MIT license).
\end{abstract}
