\begin{abstract}
Games, films and robot training need 3D worlds that are complete,
editable and owned by the people who make them. World models and
one-shot 3D generators can produce a convincing place from a sentence or
a picture, but they give frames that no engine can open, or one fused
scene without separate objects or clear rights. Studios have always
built worlds in stages, from concept art and layout to modelling,
surfacing, lighting, sound and review. We present SCORE, a world
generation framework that keeps these stages and automates them with
open vision foundation models, procedural tools and coding agents, while
the creator chooses and reviews. Every stage is a node of one runner:
its output is keyed on its inputs and its code, judged by gates that
answer pass, fail or unknown, and accepted only when nothing got worse,
so a fault is fixed in its own stage and only what follows it runs
again. Paid steps are cleared against a month's budget read from the
provider's own bill. Every model in the framework allows commercial use
of its output, so the creator stays the author of the result. We
describe the framework as built for its first world, the game world of
2099 with 18 places, and the measured gates of each of its layers:
surfaces, resting, light, motion, effects, sound and people. Results per
place are being gathered. Code and paper are available at
https://github.com/Babon-Innovations-b-v/score (MIT license).
\end{abstract}
