\begin{abstract}
Games, film, robotics and embodied AI all need 3D environments that can
be simulated, in which every object is its own body with collision, mass
and joints under the gravity of its ground, editable afterwards and
owned by whoever made it. World models give frames no simulator opens,
and one-shot 3D generators give one fused scene without separate bodies
or clear rights. SCORE is a framework that synthesises such
environments. It builds them as studios build scenes in OpenUSD, each
scene inside its parent scene and each kind of work on it (layout,
surfaces, physics, light, motion, effects, sound and people) in a layer
of its own, checked and changed apart from the rest. A creator locks the
design from the top down, first an environment concept with its style
and physics, then a concept for each scene. Open vision foundation
models, procedural tools and coding agents make every layer below, each
checked by measured gates before the next is made, and every model
allows commercial use of its output, so the creator stays the author.
Every step is keyed on its inputs, so an edit reruns only the steps
behind it. The result is one OpenUSD environment whose physics a
simulator reads as written and Newton steps with MuJoCo's solver. We
describe the framework and its method, with a first environment as the
evidence, whose four demonstrations remain gaps until its batch has run.
Each character also gets a sim body on Newton, driven by its SOMA
segment rotations. We close with the route toward bodies true to the
person and trained models that drive them. Code and paper are at
https://github.com/Babon-Innovations-b-v/score (MIT licence).
\end{abstract}
