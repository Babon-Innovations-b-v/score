\begin{abstract}
Games, films and robot training need 3D environments that are complete,
editable and owned by their makers; world models and one-shot generators
give frames no engine opens, or one fused scene without separate objects
or clear rights. Studios already build such environments by a standard:
scenes layered in OpenUSD, each scene sitting within its parent scene
and each department's work on it a layer of its own that can be checked
and changed apart from the rest. SCORE builds environments by this
convention. The creator decides from the top down: he locks an
environment concept (EC), its style and its physics, which holds the
scenes and their layering, and then a concept for each scene, in order
of parent scene and child scene. The framework makes every layer below
with open vision foundation models, procedural tools and coding agents,
each layer checked before the next is made. Every model allows
commercial use of its output, so the creator stays the author. We
describe the framework with its first environment, derived from the game
2099, as the worked example; its results are marked gaps until its
remake has run. Code and paper are available at
https://github.com/Babon-Innovations-b-v/score (MIT license).
\end{abstract}
