\section{Stages and their gates}\label{stages-and-their-gates}

\subsection{Surfaces: the finish route}\label{surfaces-the-finish-route}

Everything a scene shows comes from the creator's library, score\_lib, or is a written exception: every model, kit, finish, effect, light, sound, character, ground and algorithm (the creator's rule of 18 October 2026). Models give shape only, picture-made and code-built alike, so one style holds across them: a picture-made model keeps its geometry and its finish is replaced, never kept as the picture's own texture, its baked-in colour or a default grey. Every part takes one surface from a shared library of rule-based surfaces, written as ProcFunc functions \citep{raistrick2026procfunc} on Infinigen's shaders \citep{raistrick2023infinigen}, coloured from the scene's palette and worn only where wear has a cause (Appendix D).

The finish route gives each model its finishes (the creator's choice of 20 October 2026). Free renders of the raw model cover every face from as many angles as a coverage check needs, and each view is split into colour regions of the model's own texture. One Gemini 3.1 Pro answer a model, a scene's models in one batch, lists its finishes, each matched first to a library surface with its settings (palette token, roughness, metal, seed), and labels each region of each view. Before anything is baked, a free check compares each region with its label's surface and makes a model whose colours lie more than ΔE 32 off a gap; the labels then vote across views into one finish a face, and a face no label reaches or whose views disagree is a gap too. Each part is painted from its library surface and baked in one paid job, and one batch recheck against the close-up sorts each model into a pass or the named gap ``no library surface fits''. This route replaced the finish-map route of environment 1's first run, in which Nano Banana Pro drew the close-up again with one flat colour per finish, up to three times, and SegviGen \citep{li2026segvigen} split the mesh along that map; that route gave every piece its own rust and stains until the parts were painted from the library (Appendix A). The finish route has run its free views on two scenes; its paid steps have not run.

The bake is checked against the close-up. A finish's surface must lie within ΔE 32 of its colour there, else a nearer surface of its family is taken or a new one is proposed to the creator, and the part fails by name. From the greenhouse's water tank, which the creator found baked as three near greys and tipped, came two checks: a bake fails when it darkens a finish the close-up shows lighter, or turns all surfaces into one grey; and short legs are stretched onto one level plane, a foot over 3 cm off the floor failing. The bake's finish borders follow the model's real edges, as the cut-down copy it bakes onto is shaded smooth except at folds of 60 degrees or more.

A written exception names the scene, the object, what it is, why it is not from the library, who allowed it and when; only the creator allows one, and one without a reason or an owner refuses the whole file. A free library check traces every material, model, emitter, light, sound source and character of a scene's accepted assembly to a library entry, by content hash or a recorded origin, and the cockpit shows what does not trace as the scene's problems. It is not a gate of the completion gate, since wiring it in would move the keys of paid rows; instead the library rows at the end of every line, which hand each accepted output to the library, fail when an output is covered by no entry or written exception, so nothing reaches the library untraced. Each new entry then gets a preview clip, rendered in the batch with the films' renderer.

\subsection{Resting}\label{resting}

Loose objects are dropped onto the made ground in a physics simulation; one that would turn more than 15 degrees or drift more than 30 cm is a resting fault of the layout and keeps its laid pose. The settle stage lays where each object comes to rest as a layer (held objects snapped onto their holders, a stack onto one support, an object that would rest worse kept as laid), gated on \texttt{rested} and \texttt{resting} (no fault that appears with the layer and not without it).

\subsection{Light}\label{light}

Lamp strengths are measured, never copied from the source game. The light layer places live lamps from the scene record, with the sky and its bodies. One paid render per scene gives every lamp its own Cycles light group on the scene's views; because light adds up, the look at any strengths is mixed from these passes without rendering again, and a free sweep chooses the scale that brings the scene's brightness within 1.5 times its concept's, halving any lamp that turns what it lights white. Outdoors the suns are swept after the lamps. A lamp whose fixture stops 90 percent of its rays moves out of it, and one behind a clear lens stands just outside, as Cycles passes no light through a glowing lens.

\subsection{Motion, effects and sound}\label{motion-effects-and-sound}

\textbf{Motion.} Each moving thing (a path, a hinge, a spin, a rail, a turn, a climb) has its kind's speed range cited from real machines, gated on \texttt{clear} (no moving thing within 3 cm of a still thing or another moving thing, read every quarter second), \texttt{speeds}, \texttt{loops}, \texttt{grounded} and \texttt{covered} (every still prim of a moving kind says why). \textbf{Effects} are emitters measured against real footage (photographs of lift-offs, rover films of Martian dust devils), gated on \texttt{attached}, \texttt{footage}, \texttt{light} (the flame lights the pad at least twice as brightly as the scene's lamps) and \texttt{outside} (no open-sky particle inside a room). \textbf{Sound:} every source a scene names (room tones, objects, motions, each surface's steps) gets the environment's picked file or one MOSS-SoundEffect \citep{moss2026} take of its brief, gated on \texttt{sources}, \texttt{level}, \texttt{likeness} (CLAP \citep{wu2022clap} score at least 0.30, the tenth percentile of the environment's picks; a take under it is a gap) and \texttt{no\ freesound}.

\subsection{People}\label{people}

People are made like props: a FLUX.2 klein \citep{flux2klein} close-up in SOMA-X's bind pose, built by Pixal3D, painted by the surface line, over SOMA-X's body fitted just inside (Gauss-Newton, 24 shape numbers), whose skin weights each look point takes from its closest triangle; Kimodo's clips \citep{rempe2026kimodo}, from sentences, move it. The \textbf{shell gate} passes a look when 99 percent of its soft points lie within 5 cm of the body, its worst point recorded. Hard parts ride one joint, belts are cloth, each clip gets its own verdict (tucks, hard parts stretched, cloth or body through cloth) and casts use only passing clips; a far-only look is judged on its far body (Appendix B). \textbf{Casts:} SAM 3 finds the concept's figures, a judge tells people from pictures, reflections and empty suits, and casts are gated on \texttt{counted}, \texttt{standing}, \texttt{clear}, \texttt{made} and \texttt{drapes}; no crowd copy fills a tenth of a view's height.

\subsection{Completion}\label{completion}

A scene is complete when its gate passes on its final assembly: every row made or dropped with a reason, nothing unmade, no placeholder or resting fault, the creator's picks shown, every layer and shown scene present, nothing cited from the source game. \textbf{Accepted gaps.} A fault the creator decides to ship with is written down as an accepted gap: who decided, when, why, the objects and their measured faults. It moves only those objects' faults from the gate where they arise to the completion gate, which fails each gap by name with the numbers: a scene whose accepted gaps are its only fail is complete apart from accepted gaps, every gap listed with who decided it; any other fault leaves it not complete, and the gate is never loosened. A batch gap moves the same way, the creator's yes to the batch being its decision, and is listed with the batch run that made it. A hook stops an agent claiming a scene is done while its gate fails: agents judging their own scenes agree with a measured geometry score only 45.8 percent of the time \citep{li2026lego}.

\subsection{Prices and ledgers}\label{prices-and-ledgers}

Machines and Google calls write ledger lines with their node's clearance; the month's spend is the higher of ledgers and bill, which caught misses: EUR 1,554 billed against EUR 1,387 in the cloud ledger on 12 October 2026, and EUR 549 billed by Google for a day the earlier ledger put at EUR 30. Over 13.8 hours of environment 1's first run the ledgers hold EUR 117.45 of machines and EUR 34.19 of Google calls, most for painting (EUR 37.61), the finish maps (EUR 26.59), the people's bodies (EUR 16.09) and the light passes (EUR 10.17).

\textbf{Prices.} Every paid stage is priced from a unit cost measured on the ledgers (a close-up EUR 0.07 from 135 calls; a Pixal3D model EUR 0.11 likely and 0.20 high from 52 jobs; a preview clip EUR 0.16 from one run of six), times the units its forecast rule counts, times the bill's share over the ledger. A stage with no measured run has no price: its estimate is shown apart as a guess and never added to a price. The finish route's list and recheck are priced from the earlier route's 275 list calls at the batch tier's half price, not yet from the route itself. The batch estimate and the runner use the same unit prices, and on 21 October they agreed to the cent per stage and per model on every designed scene whose line could start. Before, the runner priced a run from a flat figure in its table, which overstated work it could carry over: the four runs of 15 October were forecast at up to EUR 72.57, 33, 70.50 and 24 and cost 10.13, 10.14, 0.67 and 0.60, while measured unit costs held (22 close-ups for the ship estimated at EUR 3.10 to 6.20, actual 3.50; seven box checks 1.75, actual 2.19; its eight shell kits 1.20, actual 0.60). One ship run spent EUR 13.58 before it stopped on missing free inputs; since then every input is checked free before a paid run.
