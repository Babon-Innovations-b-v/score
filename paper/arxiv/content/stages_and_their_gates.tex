\section{Stages and their gates}\label{stages-and-their-gates}

\subsection{Surfaces: the finish map}\label{surfaces-the-finish-map}

Everything a place shows comes from the creator's library, score\_lib, or is a written exception: every model, kit, finish, effect, light, sound, character, ground and algorithm (the creator's rule of 18 October 2026). Models give shape only, picture-made and code-built alike, so one style holds across them: a picture-made model keeps its geometry and its finish is replaced, never kept as the picture's own texture, its baked-in colour or a default grey. Every part takes one surface from a shared library of rule-based surfaces, written as ProcFunc functions \citep{raistrick2026procfunc} on Infinigen's shaders \citep{raistrick2023infinigen}, coloured from the place's palette and worn only where wear has a cause (Appendix D). Gemini 3.1 Pro \citep{gemini31pro} lists the close-up's finishes and names a library surface for each. Nano Banana Pro draws the close-up again with one flat colour per finish, up to three times until the map fits the close-up's outline, then draws unlit renders of the raw model's sides and back against it. SegviGen \citep{li2026segvigen} splits the mesh guided by the close-up's map, every map votes on the faces its view sees, and a part whose second finish holds a fifth of it fails. Each part is painted from its library surface and baked, never in the picture's colours, which gave every piece its own rust and stains (Appendix A).

The bake is checked against the close-up. A finish's surface must lie within ΔE 32 of its colour there, else a nearer surface of its family is taken or a new one is proposed to the creator, and the part fails by name. From the greenhouse's water tank, which the creator found baked as three near greys and tipped, came two checks: a bake fails when it darkens a finish the close-up shows lighter, or turns all surfaces into one grey; and short legs are stretched onto one level plane, a foot over 3 cm off the floor failing. Dirt follows the close-up.

The bake's finish borders follow the model's real edges: the cut-down copy it bakes onto is shaded smooth except at folds of 60 degrees or more, and its cage stands four voxels out, so a border no longer breaks into steps along the remeshed facets. Three free checks over each place's accepted assembly, outside the gates, measure texture detail near the cameras against the room's kit, how ragged each part's finish borders are, and kit bakes older than their bake code; the hangar's ship fails, with 56 pixels a metre at 0.5 m against its kit's 617. A written exception names the place, the object, what it is, why it is not from the library, who allowed it and when; only the creator allows one, and one without a reason or an owner refuses the whole file. A free library check traces every material, model, emitter, light, sound source and character of a place's accepted assembly to a library entry, by content hash or a recorded origin, and lists what does not trace. It is not a gate of any row yet, since wiring it in would move the keys of paid rows; the creator cockpit shows its findings as the place's problems.

\subsection{Resting}\label{resting}

Loose objects are dropped onto the made ground in a physics simulation; one that would turn more than 15 degrees or drift more than 30 cm is a resting fault of the layout and keeps its laid pose. The settle stage lays where each object comes to rest as a layer (held objects snapped onto their holders, a stack onto one support, an object that would rest worse kept as laid), gated on \texttt{rested} and \texttt{resting} (no fault that appears with the layer and not without it).

\subsection{Light}\label{light}

Lamp strengths are measured, never copied from the source game. The light layer places live lamps from the scene record, with the sky, the sun and the Earth. One paid render per place gives every lamp its own Cycles light group on the place's views; because light adds up, the look at any strengths is mixed from these passes without rendering again, and a free sweep chooses the scale that brings the place's brightness within 1.5 times its concept's, halving any lamp that turns what it lights white. Outdoors the suns are swept after the lamps. A lamp whose fixture stops 90 percent of its rays moves out of it, and one behind a clear lens stands just outside, as Cycles passes no light through a glowing lens.

\subsection{Motion, effects and sound}\label{motion-effects-and-sound}

\textbf{Motion.} Each moving thing (a path, a hinge, a spin, a rail, a turn, a climb) has its kind's speed range cited from real machines, gated on \texttt{clear} (no moving thing within 3 cm of a still thing or another moving thing, read every quarter second), \texttt{speeds}, \texttt{loops}, \texttt{grounded} and \texttt{covered} (every still prim of a moving kind says why). \textbf{Effects} are emitters measured against real footage (photographs of lift-offs, rover films of Martian dust devils), gated on \texttt{attached}, \texttt{footage}, \texttt{light} (the flame lights the pad at least twice as brightly as the place's lamps) and \texttt{outside} (no open-sky particle inside a room). \textbf{Sound:} every source a place names (room tones, objects, motions, each surface's steps) gets the environment's picked file or the best of eight MOSS-SoundEffect \citep{moss2026} takes of its brief, gated on \texttt{sources}, \texttt{level}, \texttt{likeness} (CLAP \citep{wu2022clap} score at least 0.30, the tenth percentile of the environment's picks) and \texttt{no\ freesound}.

\subsection{People}\label{people}

People are made like props: a FLUX.2 klein \citep{flux2klein} close-up in SOMA-X's bind pose, built by Pixal3D, painted by the surface line, over SOMA-X's body fitted just inside (Gauss-Newton, 24 shape numbers), whose skin weights each look point takes from its closest triangle; Kimodo's clips \citep{rempe2026kimodo}, from sentences, move it. The \textbf{shell gate} passes a look when 99 percent of its soft points lie within 5 cm of the body, its worst point recorded. Hard parts ride one joint, belts are cloth, each clip gets its own verdict (tucks, hard parts stretched, cloth or body through cloth) and casts use only passing clips; a far-only look is judged on its far body (Appendix B). \textbf{Casts:} SAM 3 \citep{carion2025sam3} finds the concept's figures, a judge tells people from pictures, reflections and empty suits, and casts are gated on \texttt{counted}, \texttt{standing}, \texttt{clear}, \texttt{made} and \texttt{drapes}; no crowd copy fills a tenth of a view's height.

\subsection{Completion}\label{completion}

A place is complete when its gate passes on its final assembly: every row made or dropped with a reason, nothing unmade, no placeholder or resting fault, the creator's picks shown, every layer and shown place present, nothing cited from the source game. \textbf{Accepted gaps.} A fault the creator decides to ship with today is written down as an accepted gap: who decided, when, why, the objects and their measured faults. It moves only those objects' faults from the gate where they arise to the completion gate, which fails each gap by name with the numbers: a place whose accepted gaps are its only fail is complete apart from accepted gaps, every gap listed with who decided it; any other fault leaves it not complete, and the gate is never loosened. A cast's person with no accepted body is an accepted gap the same way. A hook stops an agent claiming a place is done while its gate fails: agents judging their own scenes agree with a measured geometry score only 45.8 percent of the time \citep{li2026lego}.

\subsection{Budget and ledgers}\label{budget-and-ledgers}

Machines and Google calls write ledger lines with their node's ticket; the month's spend is the higher of ledgers and bill, which caught misses: EUR 1,554 billed against EUR 1,387 in the cloud ledger on 12 October 2026, and EUR 549 billed by Google for a day the earlier ledger put at EUR 30. Over 13.8 hours of environment 1's run the ledgers hold EUR 117.45 of machines and EUR 34.19 of Google calls, most for painting (EUR 37.61), the finish maps (EUR 26.59), the people's bodies (EUR 16.09) and the light passes (EUR 10.17).

\textbf{Forecast.} Before a paid piece runs, its estimate is written down with the measured unit cost it rests on (the ledgers' medians per node, model or picture); after it, the ledger's actual. The projected total for the month is reported against a hard stop the creator sets. The record shows which estimates to trust: measured unit costs held (22 close-ups for the ship estimated at EUR 3.10 to 6.20, actual 3.50; seven box checks 1.75, actual 2.19; the ship's six rooms 27.40, actual about 16.70; its eight shell kits 1.20, actual 0.60), while the runner's flat table figure overstated work it could carry over (the prologue's surfaces and light, EUR 101.50 in the table, cost 0.86). The runner's forecast for a run is a ceiling, not an estimate: the four runs of 15 October were forecast at up to EUR 72.57, 33, 70.50 and 24 and cost 10.13, 10.14, 0.67 and 0.60, the last two stopped early because they ran older code. One ship run spent EUR 13.58 before it stopped on missing free inputs; the rule since then is a free pass over every input before a paid run. The bill runs about 21 percent over the ledgers, so the forecast multiplies every ledger-priced estimate by 1.21; on 15 October it put the month at EUR 495 to 507 against the creator's hard stop of EUR 510.

\textbf{Held and run, 15 October.} Before the night's runs the runner listed every stale node with its price. All people work was held for next month, EUR 89.50: 23 bodies (EUR 56.50, 6 to be made again after changes to the people maker, 17 not made yet), 12 takes (EUR 18), their maps, names, parts and paint (EUR 14) and one likeness calibration (EUR 1). Three review renders (EUR 14) were left out, as neither the completion gate nor the films read them. The place films, EUR 0.70 each, have not run.
