\section{Stages and their gates}\label{stages-and-their-gates}

\subsection{Surfaces: the finish route}\label{surfaces-the-finish-route}

Everything a scene shows comes from the creator's library, score\_lib, or is a written exception (the creator's rule of 18 October 2026). Models give shape only: a picture-made model keeps its geometry and its finish is replaced. Every part takes one surface from a shared library of rule-based surfaces, written as ProcFunc functions \citep{raistrick2026procfunc} on Infinigen's shaders \citep{raistrick2023infinigen}, coloured from the scene's palette and worn only where wear has a cause (Appendix D).

The finish route gives each model its finishes (the creator's choice of 20 October 2026). Free renders cover every face of the raw model, and each view is split into colour regions. One Gemini 3.1 Pro answer a model lists its finishes, each matched first to a library surface with its settings, and labels each region of each view; the labels vote across views into one finish a face, and a face no label reaches or whose views disagree is a gap. Each part is painted from its library surface and baked, and the bake is checked against the close-up: a finish's surface must lie within ΔE 32 of its colour there, a bake fails when it darkens a finish the close-up shows lighter or turns all surfaces into one grey, and a foot over 3 cm off the floor fails. A free library check traces every material, model, light, sound and character of a scene's accepted assembly to a library entry or a written exception.

\subsection{Resting}\label{resting}

Loose objects are dropped onto the made ground in a physics simulation; one that would turn more than 15 degrees or drift more than 30 cm is a resting fault of the layout and keeps its laid pose. The settle stage lays where each object comes to rest as a layer (held objects snapped onto their holders, a stack onto one support, an object that would rest worse kept as laid), gated on \texttt{rested} and \texttt{resting} (no fault that appears with the layer and not without it).

\subsection{Light}\label{light}

Lamp strengths are measured, never copied from the source game. The light layer places live lamps from the scene record, with the sky and its bodies. One paid render per scene gives every lamp its own Cycles light group on the scene's views; because light adds up, the look at any strengths is mixed from these passes without rendering again, and a free sweep chooses the scale that brings the scene's brightness within 1.5 times its concept's, halving any lamp that turns what it lights white.

\subsection{Motion, effects and sound}\label{motion-effects-and-sound}

\textbf{Motion.} Each moving thing (a path, a hinge, a spin, a rail, a turn, a climb) has its kind's speed range cited from real machines, gated on \texttt{clear} (no moving thing within 3 cm of a still thing or another moving thing, read every quarter second), \texttt{speeds}, \texttt{loops}, \texttt{grounded} and \texttt{covered}. \textbf{Effects} are emitters measured against real footage, gated on \texttt{attached}, \texttt{footage}, \texttt{light} and \texttt{outside} (no open-sky particle inside a room). \textbf{Sound:} every source a scene names gets the environment's picked file or one MOSS-SoundEffect \citep{moss2026} take of its brief, gated on \texttt{sources}, \texttt{level}, \texttt{likeness} (CLAP \citep{wu2022clap} score at least 0.30; a take under it is a gap) and \texttt{no\ freesound}.

\subsection{People}\label{people}

People are made like props: a FLUX.2 klein \citep{flux2klein} close-up in SOMA-X's bind pose, built by Pixal3D, painted by the surface line, over SOMA-X's body fitted just inside, whose skin weights each look point takes from its closest triangle; Kimodo's clips \citep{rempe2026kimodo}, from sentences, move it. The \textbf{shell gate} passes a look when 99 percent of its soft points lie within 5 cm of the body, its worst point recorded; each clip gets its own verdict (tucks, hard parts stretched, cloth or body through cloth), and casts use only passing clips. \textbf{Casts:} each person of a scene's inventory is a look of the people line, SAM 3 finds the concept's figures, and casts are gated on \texttt{counted}, \texttt{standing}, \texttt{clear}, \texttt{made} and \texttt{drapes}.

\subsection{Completion}\label{completion}

A scene is complete when its gate passes on its final assembly: every row made or dropped with a reason, nothing unmade, no placeholder or resting fault, the creator's picks shown, every layer and shown scene present, nothing cited from the source game. A fault the creator decides to ship with is an accepted gap, written with who decided, when, why and its measured faults; the completion gate lists every gap by name and is never loosened, and a scene whose accepted gaps are its only fail is complete apart from accepted gaps. A hook stops an agent claiming a scene is done while its gate fails: agents judging their own scenes agree with a measured geometry score only 45.8 percent of the time \citep{li2026lego}.

\subsection{Prices and ledgers}\label{prices-and-ledgers}

Every paid stage is priced from a unit cost measured on the ledgers, times the units its forecast rule counts, times the bill's share over the ledger; a stage with no measured run has no price, and its estimate is shown apart as a guess. Machines and Google calls write ledger lines with their node's clearance, and the month's spend is the higher of ledgers and bill, which caught misses: EUR 1,554 billed against EUR 1,387 in the cloud ledger on 12 October 2026, and EUR 549 billed by Google for a day the earlier ledger put at EUR 30. A paid node runs only when every gate on its inputs passed, the month's books are known and its batch's stop has room for its forecast.
