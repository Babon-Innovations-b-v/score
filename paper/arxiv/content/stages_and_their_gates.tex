\section{Stages and their gates}\label{stages-and-their-gates}

\subsection{Surfaces: the finish map}\label{surfaces-the-finish-map}

Models give shape only; every part takes one surface from a shared library of rule-based surfaces, written as ProcFunc functions \citep{raistrick2026procfunc} on Infinigen's shaders \citep{raistrick2023infinigen}, coloured from the place's palette and worn only where wear has a cause (Appendix D). Gemini 3.1 Pro \citep{gemini31pro} lists the close-up's finishes and names a library surface for each. Nano Banana Pro draws the close-up again with one flat colour per finish, up to three times until the map fits the close-up's outline, then draws unlit renders of the raw model's sides and back against that map. SegviGen \citep{li2026segvigen} splits the mesh guided by the close-up's map, every map votes on the faces its view sees, and a part whose second finish holds a fifth of it fails. Each part is painted from its library surface and baked; the picture's own colours are never kept, because when every piece kept them, every piece brought its own rust and stains (Appendix A).

The bake is checked against the close-up. A finish's surface must lie within ΔE 32 of its colour there, else a nearer surface of its family is taken or a new one is proposed to the creator, and the part fails by name. Two checks came from the greenhouse's water tank, which the creator found baked as three near greys and standing tipped: a bake fails when it turns a finish the close-up shows lighter into a darker one, or all its surfaces into one grey the close-up does not have; and a model on legs has its short legs stretched onto one level plane before its bake, a foot more than 3 cm off the floor failing. Each model is dirtied as much as its close-up shows, capped at its place's dirt.

\subsection{Resting}\label{resting}

Loose objects are dropped onto the made ground in a physics simulation; one that would turn more than 15 degrees or drift more than 30 cm is a resting fault of the layout and keeps its laid pose. The settle stage lays where each object comes to rest as a layer of its own (held objects snapped onto their holders, a stack onto one support, an object that would rest worse kept as laid). Its gates are \texttt{rested} and \texttt{resting} (no fault that appears with the layer and not without it).

\subsection{Light}\label{light}

Lamp strengths are measured, never copied from the source game. The light layer places live lamps where the scene record puts them, with the sky, the sun and, seen from the Moon, the Earth. One paid render per place gives every lamp its own Cycles light group on the place's views; because light adds up, the look at any strengths is mixed from these passes without rendering again, and a free sweep chooses the scale that brings the place's brightness within 1.5 times its concept's, halving any lamp that turns what it lights white. Under the open sky the suns are swept after the lamps. A lamp is measured where it shines: one whose fixture stops 90 percent of its rays moves out of the fixture, and one behind a clear lens stands just outside it, since Cycles passes no light through a lens that also glows. Baking a pass per lamp, mixed live in the engine, is planned.

\subsection{Motion, effects and sound}\label{motion-effects-and-sound}

\textbf{Motion.} Each moving thing is a mover in the place's record (a path, a hinge, a spin, a rail, a turn, a climb) with its kind's speed range cited from real machines. The gates read the composed stage every quarter second: \texttt{clear} (no mover within 3 cm of a still thing or another mover), \texttt{speeds}, \texttt{loops}, \texttt{grounded} and \texttt{covered} (every prim of a moving kind that stays still says why).

\textbf{Effects} are emitters on a prim, measured against real footage: the launch's cloud and flame in rocket heights from photographs of real lift-offs, the camp's dust devils from rover films of Mars. The gates are \texttt{attached}, \texttt{footage} (every measure of the baked particles within the range read off footage), \texttt{light} (the flame lights the pad at least twice as brightly as the place's lamps) and \texttt{outside} (no open-sky particle inside a room).

\textbf{Sound.} Every source a place names (room tones, objects, motions, each surface's steps and impacts) gets the world's picked file or the best of eight MOSS-SoundEffect \citep{moss2026} takes of its brief, gated on \texttt{sources}, \texttt{level} (loudness, peak and length), \texttt{likeness} (CLAP \citep{wu2022clap} score at least 0.30, the tenth percentile of the world's picks) and \texttt{no\ freesound}.

\subsection{People}\label{people}

People are made like props. A close-up of the dressed person in SOMA-X's bind pose, drawn by FLUX.2 klein \citep{flux2klein}, is built by Pixal3D and painted by the surface line. SOMA-X's body is fitted just inside the look (Gauss-Newton on 24 shape numbers), and every point of the look takes the skin weights of its closest body triangle, so Kimodo's clips \citep{rempe2026kimodo}, written from sentences, move it. The \textbf{shell gate} passes a look when 99 percent of its soft points lie within 5 cm of the body, its worst point recorded. Hard parts ride one joint each (hair, boots, bare hands, helmets), and belts are worn as cloth. Each clip gets its own verdict (tucks, hard parts stretched, cloth through cloth, body through cloth), and a cast uses only the clips that pass. A look seen only far away is judged on its far body, and a look's colour comes from the cast record's words (Appendix B).

\textbf{Casts.} SAM 3 \citep{carion2025sam3} finds the figures on a place's views, and a vision-language judge tells a person from a picture, a reflection or an empty suit. A cast is gated on \texttt{counted}, \texttt{standing}, \texttt{clear}, \texttt{made} and \texttt{drapes}; a crowd stands only where no copy fills a tenth of a view's height, and every person wears their look's accepted body, never a stand-in.

\subsection{Completion}\label{completion}

A place is complete when its completion gate passes on its final assembly: every inventory row has its object or a written reason; nothing visible is unmade or a code placeholder; no resting fault; every pick of the creator is the model shown; and the place holds what it needs: its cast in made looks, its effects and motions, its crops, every generated model held to its close-up and repainted, every lamp and room tone of its record, every place it shows, and nothing copied from the source game. Each answer is tied to a fingerprint of the stage; an answer made on another stage is unknown, and a hook refuses to end an agent's session that claims a place is done while its gate fails. Coding agents judging their own scenes agree with a measured score on geometry only 45.8 percent of the time \citep{li2026lego}, so no agent's word completes a place.

\subsection{Budget and ledgers}\label{budget-and-ledgers}

Every rented machine writes a ledger line with its euros, and every call to Google's models writes a started line before the call and a priced line after it, so a call that never returns still counts. Each line carries the ticket of its node. The month's spend is the higher of the ledgers and the provider's bill, because the ledgers missed costs: on 12 October 2026 the month's cloud bill stood at EUR 1,554 against EUR 1,387 in the ledger (disks and addresses billed by the hour, storage, some card time), and on 11 October Google billed EUR 549 for a day in which the earlier ledger held EUR 30. Over 13.8 hours of world 1's run from the first ticketed line, the ledgers hold EUR 117.45 of rented machines and EUR 34.19 of Google's models; the largest stages were painting (EUR 37.61), the finish maps (EUR 26.59), the people's bodies (EUR 16.09) and the light passes (EUR 10.17).
