\section{Stages and their gates}\label{stages-and-their-gates}

\subsection{Surfaces: the finish map}\label{surfaces-the-finish-map}

Models give shape only; every part takes one surface from a shared library of rule-based surfaces, written as ProcFunc functions \citep{raistrick2026procfunc} on Infinigen's shaders \citep{raistrick2023infinigen}, coloured from the place's palette and worn only where wear has a cause (Appendix D). Gemini 3.1 Pro \citep{gemini31pro} lists the close-up's finishes and names a library surface for each. Nano Banana Pro draws the close-up again with one flat colour per finish, up to three times until the map fits the close-up's outline, then draws unlit renders of the raw model's sides and back against it. SegviGen \citep{li2026segvigen} splits the mesh guided by the close-up's map, every map votes on the faces its view sees, and a part whose second finish holds a fifth of it fails. Each part is painted from its library surface and baked, never in the picture's colours, which gave every piece its own rust and stains (Appendix A).

The bake is checked against the close-up. A finish's surface must lie within ΔE 32 of its colour there, else a nearer surface of its family is taken or a new one is proposed to the creator, and the part fails by name. From the greenhouse's water tank, which the creator found baked as three near greys and tipped, came two checks: a bake fails when it darkens a finish the close-up shows lighter, or turns all surfaces into one grey; and short legs are stretched onto one level plane, a foot over 3 cm off the floor failing. Dirt follows the close-up.

\subsection{Resting}\label{resting}

Loose objects are dropped onto the made ground in a physics simulation; one that would turn more than 15 degrees or drift more than 30 cm is a resting fault of the layout and keeps its laid pose. The settle stage lays where each object comes to rest as a layer (held objects snapped onto their holders, a stack onto one support, an object that would rest worse kept as laid), gated on \texttt{rested} and \texttt{resting} (no fault that appears with the layer and not without it).

\subsection{Light}\label{light}

Lamp strengths are measured, never copied from the source game. The light layer places live lamps from the scene record, with the sky, the sun and the Earth. One paid render per place gives every lamp its own Cycles light group on the place's views; because light adds up, the look at any strengths is mixed from these passes without rendering again, and a free sweep chooses the scale that brings the place's brightness within 1.5 times its concept's, halving any lamp that turns what it lights white. Outdoors the suns are swept after the lamps. A lamp whose fixture stops 90 percent of its rays moves out of it, and one behind a clear lens stands just outside, as Cycles passes no light through a glowing lens.

\subsection{Motion, effects and sound}\label{motion-effects-and-sound}

\textbf{Motion.} Each mover (a path, a hinge, a spin, a rail, a turn, a climb) has its kind's speed range cited from real machines, gated on \texttt{clear} (no mover within 3 cm of a still thing or another mover, read every quarter second), \texttt{speeds}, \texttt{loops}, \texttt{grounded} and \texttt{covered} (every still prim of a moving kind says why). \textbf{Effects} are emitters measured against real footage (photographs of lift-offs, rover films of Martian dust devils), gated on \texttt{attached}, \texttt{footage}, \texttt{light} (the flame lights the pad at least twice as brightly as the place's lamps) and \texttt{outside} (no open-sky particle inside a room). \textbf{Sound:} every source a place names (room tones, objects, motions, each surface's steps) gets the world's picked file or the best of eight MOSS-SoundEffect \citep{moss2026} takes of its brief, gated on \texttt{sources}, \texttt{level}, \texttt{likeness} (CLAP \citep{wu2022clap} score at least 0.30, the tenth percentile of the world's picks) and \texttt{no\ freesound}.

\subsection{People}\label{people}

People are made like props. A close-up of the dressed person in SOMA-X's bind pose, drawn by FLUX.2 klein \citep{flux2klein}, is built by Pixal3D and painted by the surface line. SOMA-X's body is fitted just inside the look (Gauss-Newton on 24 shape numbers), and every point of the look takes the skin weights of its closest body triangle, so Kimodo's clips \citep{rempe2026kimodo}, written from sentences, move it. The \textbf{shell gate} passes a look when 99 percent of its soft points lie within 5 cm of the body, its worst point recorded. Hard parts ride one joint each, belts are worn as cloth, and each clip gets its own verdict (tucks, hard parts stretched, cloth or body through cloth); a cast uses only the clips that pass. A look seen only far away is judged on its far body (Appendix B). \textbf{Casts:} SAM 3 \citep{carion2025sam3} finds the figures on a place's views, a vision-language judge tells a person from a picture, a reflection or an empty suit, and a cast is gated on \texttt{counted}, \texttt{standing}, \texttt{clear}, \texttt{made} and \texttt{drapes}; a crowd stands only where no copy fills a tenth of a view's height.

\textbf{Results so far (experimental).} On 13 October four looks passed every body gate (joins, outline, likeness, triangles, shell) and a fifth failed on triangles, but few of their clips are usable (Table 2). Two faults run through every clip of Nev and Sefa before any motion: a boot's tuck band lies 9 to 11 mm outside the trouser leg at the bind pose. Scored only on what is drawn (the bands left out), no clip of any look is usable: where hair or cloth meets the head or hand skin the two sides are weighted differently and open as the joints move (cloth seams never open), and the settled cloth crosses itself beyond the bind. Openings over 10 cm on three looks are not yet confirmed by a render. Binding the take as one closed mesh removes the openings, but the player's limbs still sink into the chest and hips at the bends: in every clip 87 to 563 crossing points are visible from the places' cameras and close views. Pushing points out of the look's own volume, limiting the offset from SOMA-X at the armpits and inner thighs, fold-centred weights and dual-quaternion skinning each left that count the same or higher. The renders trace it to the take itself: the close-up's arms and thighs lie against the body, so Pixal3D builds the armpit and groin as one surface, which stretches when a limb moves. The clips' joint angles stay within the normal human ranges of the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons, except hip extension of 24 and 27 degrees (limit 20) in two clips.

\subsection{Completion}\label{completion}

A place is complete when its gate passes on its final assembly: every row has its object or a reason; nothing visible is unmade or a placeholder; no resting fault; the creator's picks are shown; and it holds its cast in made looks, its effects, motions and crops, every generated model repainted and held to its close-up, every lamp and room tone, every place it shows, and nothing from the source game. A hook stops an agent claiming a place is done while its gate fails: agents judging their own scenes agree with a measured geometry score only 45.8 percent of the time \citep{li2026lego}.

\subsection{Budget and ledgers}\label{budget-and-ledgers}

Every rented machine, and every Google call before and after it, writes a ledger line with its node's ticket. The month's spend is the higher of the ledgers and the bill, which caught what they missed: on 12 October 2026 the cloud bill stood at EUR 1,554 against EUR 1,387 in the ledger (disks, addresses, storage), and on 11 October Google billed EUR 549 for a day the earlier ledger put at EUR 30. Over 13.8 hours of world 1's run the ledgers hold EUR 117.45 of machines and EUR 34.19 of Google calls, most for painting (EUR 37.61), the finish maps (EUR 26.59), the people's bodies (EUR 16.09) and the light passes (EUR 10.17).
