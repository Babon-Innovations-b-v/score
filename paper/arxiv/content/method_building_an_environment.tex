\section{Method: building an environment}\label{method-building-an-environment}

\subsection{From concept to plan}\label{from-concept-to-plan}

\textbf{The environment concept.} The creator first locks the EC, which fixes the environment's visual style and physical setting together with his reference images and the scenes it contains, in a short structured interview conducted with the vision-language model Gemini 3.1 Pro \citep{gemini31pro}. An environment plan then places the site of each top scene on that ground and computes the sky visible from it.

\textbf{A concept for each scene, top down.} A concept image is then locked for each scene, parents before their child scenes. Each candidate image is one call to the image generation model Nano Banana Pro \citep{nanobananapro}, made on a concept review page where the creator marks regions and requests a new image. A child scene is drawn only once its parent has been locked and planned, from a crop of the parent's concept, so that it fits the parent it stands in.

\textbf{Inventory and plan.} Gemini 3.1 Pro lists each object of the locked concept as a row of the scene inventory, giving its kind, count, anchor and size in metres, and the segmentation model SAM 3 \citep{carion2025sam3} localises each row with a bounding box. Nothing outside the inventory is generated or placed. Nano Banana Pro then draws the scene from directly above, and Gemini 3.1 Pro writes the plan in metres from that drawing, the concept and the inventory. The plan gives the outline, walls, openings, levels, stairs, roofs and terrain, each item with its orientation, people as the spots on which they stand, and the rooms of the child scenes. Free checks, which make no paid model call, test that every row is placed as many times as its count, inside the outline and clear of other floor items, that every door and standing spot can be reached on foot, and that the child scenes tile their parent. Any remaining violations are returned to the model as notes at most twice, and only what still fails is recorded as a gap. The creator's edits on the plan review page always take precedence and cause everything that follows to be recomputed.

\textbf{Shapes first.} The route follows one coarse-to-fine loop at every scale. A parent scene receives its concept, plan and a rough block model, and its plan is divided into the shapes of its child scenes. Once those shapes are fixed, all child scenes are produced in parallel, each repeating the loop. The stages \texttt{rough-model} and \texttt{shell-split} are implemented but have not yet run.

\subsection{Layers from row kinds}\label{layers-from-row-kinds}

A scene's layers are derived from its design alone. Each inventory row's kind, determined from the noun in the inventory reading by a single table of rules, states what its objects do, whether they emit, move, lie loose, glow, grow, act or sound. A scene receives an effects, motion, physics, crops or people layer only for rows that do so, and always a light and a sound layer. Each layer is generated from its rows by the rule of their kind, so a new environment needs no hand-written list of layers. The scene record holds what no generated object covers (building shells, stairs, ground and water, the distant backdrop, lamps and the neighbouring scenes its doorways lead to), and its gate requires the shell to lie within 5 cm of the plan and every doorway to open onto a floor.

\textbf{Models.} Objects follow the prop route first, which turns a single image of an object into a 3D model. Nano Banana Pro draws a close-up of the object from its crop of the concept and redraws it at most three times when the shape check rejects it. The image-to-3D model Pixal3D \citep{li2026pixal3d} then reconstructs the mesh, and a likeness gate compares its silhouettes from eight viewpoints with the close-up. Only an object whose checks fail is built instead by a procedural builder, a program that a coding agent writes, and a thin straight object (a pipe, post or beam) whose every close-up failed is generated in code by sweeping its cross-section along its length.

\textbf{Surfaces.} Everything a scene shows comes from the creator's asset library, score\_lib, or is recorded as a written exception. A generated model contributes geometry only, and each of its parts takes one material from a library of procedural materials, written as ProcFunc functions \citep{raistrick2026procfunc} on Infinigen's shaders \citep{raistrick2023infinigen} and coloured from the scene's palette. The finish route labels each face of the model from free renders, matches each finish to a library material, bakes it into textures and checks the bake against the close-up, requiring each finish to lie within a colour difference of ΔE 32 of its colour there. A free library check traces every material, model, light, sound and character of an accepted assembly back to the library or to an exception.

\textbf{Settle and physics.} Loose objects are dropped onto the generated ground in a rigid-body simulation, the settle, and an object that would rotate by more than 15 degrees or drift by more than 30 cm is recorded as a resting fault and keeps its laid-out pose. The settle writes the pose in which each object comes to rest as a layer. The physics trial then steps the composed scene in Newton, using MuJoCo's solver with Newton's contact handling under the gravity of the body the scene stands on, and evaluates it with the gates \texttt{reacted}, \texttt{at\ rest}, \texttt{energy} (no increase in the energy of undriven bodies) and \texttt{hinge} (a hinged door leaf swings to its joint limit). The trial moved from Blender's Bullet engine \citep{bullet} to Newton on 27 October 2026. On a test scene at lunar gravity (two dropped crates, a driven door with a wheel fixed to it and a pushed stool), both engines gave the same result on every gate, and Newton brought the crates to rest on their targets where Bullet left them standing off by its contact margin of 8 mm. Newton's deterministic mode, which reproduced bitwise-identical results over six runs of that test scene, cannot handle scenes with many loose bodies, because its buffers exceed the 32-bit limit, so the trial and the settle run without it. Two runs of a 25-body test scene then agree within 5e-7 m for every body except one that falls off the edge of the floor. The settle moves to Newton in the same way, so that one engine steps both what comes to rest and what is tested, and one effect is simulated in Newton as a first case for the effects layer.

\textbf{Light, motion, effects and sound.} The sky is computed from the physical setting in the EC, never drawn, with each celestial body at its true direction, apparent size and phase as seen from the scene's site. The lamps are the inventory's glowing rows. One render per scene places each lamp in its own light group, and a free sweep mixes their intensities to bring the scene's brightness within a factor of 1.5 of its concept's. Every object of a moving row moves by the rule of its kind at a speed taken from cited real machines, and is gated on nothing coming within 3 cm of anything else. Emitters are measured against real footage. Each sound source receives a library file or one take generated by the text-to-audio model MOSS-SoundEffect \citep{moss2026}, gated on an audio-text similarity score from CLAP \citep{wu2022clap} of at least 0.30.

\textbf{People.} People are models like any other, each made from a close-up per person row, reconstructed by Pixal3D and given its materials by the finish route. The SOMA parametric body model \citep{saito2026soma}, estimated from the close-up by SAM 3D Body \citep{yang2026sam3dbody}, is fitted beneath the model as its skeletal rig and animated with motion clips from Kimodo \citep{rempe2026kimodo}. A shell gate accepts a clothed appearance when 99 percent of the points of its non-rigid parts lie within 5 cm of the body. Each person stands on a person spot of its scene's plan.

\textbf{Sim bodies.} Each character also receives a sim body, its physical twin in Newton, so that the people in an environment are physically simulated as well as animated. The minimal body is an articulated rigid-body model built directly from SOMA's 23 segments, with a ball joint at each SOMA joint frame. It is driven by the character's SOMA segment rotations without scaling or per-person setup, its mass is distributed according to its segment volumes, and it stands on a SCORE floor so that its ground reaction forces arise from contact. Muscles are added as a minimal first step. Built this way, the body follows SOMA's rotations to within 6.9e-9 m, and in Newton it matches plain MuJoCo to within 3.4e-8 after 200 steps.

\textbf{Completion.} A scene is complete when its completion gate passes on its final assembly, with every inventory row made or dropped with a reason, nothing left unmade, no resting fault, and every layer and every visible scene present. A fault the creator chooses to ship with is an accepted gap, recorded with who decided, when and why. The gate lists every gap by name and is never relaxed. A hook prevents a coding agent from claiming that a scene is done while its gate fails, because agents judging their own scenes agree with a measured geometry score only 45.8 percent of the time \citep{li2026lego}. The films of a complete scene are rendered from scripts the creator has locked.
