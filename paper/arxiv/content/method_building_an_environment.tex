\section{Method: building an environment}\label{method-building-an-environment}

\subsection{From concept to plan}\label{from-concept-to-plan}

\textbf{The environment concept.} The creator first locks the EC in a short interview with Gemini 3.1 Pro \citep{gemini31pro}: its style and its physics, with his reference pictures, and the scenes it holds. An environment plan places each top scene's site on that ground and works out the sky each site sees.

\textbf{A concept for each scene, top down.} For each scene a concept is then locked, in order of parent scene and child scene. Each draw is one Nano Banana Pro \citep{nanobananapro} call, on a concept review page where he marks areas and asks for a new draw. A child scene is drawn only once its parent is locked and planned, on a crop of the parent's concept, so it fits the parent it stands in.

\textbf{Inventory and plan.} Gemini 3.1 Pro lists every object of the locked concept as a row of the scene inventory, with its kind, count, anchor and size in metres, and SAM 3 \citep{carion2025sam3} boxes each row; nothing is made or placed that is not a row. Nano Banana Pro then draws the scene from straight above, and Gemini 3.1 Pro writes the plan in metres from that drawing, the concept and the inventory: outline, walls, openings, levels, stairs, roofs, terrain, each item with its facing, people as the spots they stand on, and the child scenes' rooms. Free checks follow (every row placed as often as it counts, inside the outline, no two floor items in each other, every door and standing spot reached on foot, the child scenes tiling their parent); what remains goes back as notes at most twice, and only what is left is a gap. The creator's edits on the plan review page always win and redo what follows.

\textbf{Shapes first.} The route is one loop at every scale: a parent scene gets its concept, plan and a rough model; its plan is split into the shapes of its child scenes, and once those shapes are fixed every child scene is made in parallel, each repeating the loop. The rows \texttt{rough-model} and \texttt{shell-split} are built and have not run.

\subsection{Layers from row kinds}\label{layers-from-row-kinds}

A scene's layers are read off its design alone: each inventory row's kind, found from the reading's noun by one table of rules, says what its objects do (emit, move, lie loose, glow, grow, act, sound). A scene gets an effects, motion, physics, crops or people layer only for rows that do so, and light and sound always; each layer is made from the rows by their kind's rule, so a new environment needs no hand-written list. The scene record holds what no made piece covers (shells, stairs, ground and water, backdrop, lamps, neighbours), gated on the shell lying within 5 cm of the plan and every doorway opening onto a floor.

\textbf{Models.} Every object goes the prop route first: Nano Banana Pro draws its close-up from its crop of the concept (redrawn at most three times when the shape check rejects it), Pixal3D \citep{li2026pixal3d} builds it, and a likeness gate compares its outline from eight sides with the close-up. Only an object whose checks fail is built by a code builder a coding agent writes; a thin straight thing (a pipe, post or beam) whose every close-up failed is swept along its length in code.

\textbf{Surfaces.} Everything a scene shows comes from the creator's library, score\_lib, or is a written exception. A model gives shape only: each part takes one surface from a library of rule-based surfaces, written as ProcFunc functions \citep{raistrick2026procfunc} on Infinigen's shaders \citep{raistrick2023infinigen} and coloured from the scene's palette. The finish route labels each face from free renders of the model, matches each finish to a library surface, bakes it and checks the bake against the close-up (each finish within ΔE 32 of its colour there). A free library check traces every material, model, light, sound and character of an accepted assembly to the library or an exception.

\textbf{Settle and physics.} Loose objects are dropped onto the made ground; one that would turn more than 15 degrees or drift more than 30 cm is a resting fault and keeps its laid pose. The settle lays where each object comes to rest as a layer. The physics trial then steps the composed scene on Newton with MuJoCo's solver on Newton's contacts, under the gravity of the body the scene stands on, and judges it by gates: \texttt{reacted}, \texttt{at\ rest}, \texttt{energy} (no rise in undriven energy) and \texttt{hinge} (a hinged leaf swings to its limit). The trial moved from Blender's Bullet \citep{bullet} to Newton on 27 October 2026. On a toy scene at Moon gravity (two crate drops, a driven door with a wheel fixed to it, a pushed stool), both engines gave the same answers on every gate, and Newton landed the crates on their targets where Bullet left its contact margins of 8 mm; Newton's deterministic mode, bitwise equal over six runs on that toy, cannot hold scenes with many loose bodies (its buffers pass the 32-bit limit), so the trial and the settle run without it; two runs of a 25-body toy then agree within 5e-7 m on every body but one that falls off the floor's edge. The settle moves to Newton the same way, so one engine steps what comes to rest and what is tried, and one effect is stepped on Newton as a first case for the effects layer.

\textbf{Light, motion, effects and sound.} The sky is worked out from the EC's physics, never drawn: each body at its true direction, size and phase as seen from the scene's site. The lamps are the inventory's glowing rows; one render per scene gives each lamp its own light group, and a free sweep mixes their strengths to bring the scene within 1.5 times its concept's brightness. Every object of a moving row moves by its kind's rule at a speed cited from real machines, gated on nothing passing within 3 cm of another thing; emitters are measured against real footage; each sound source gets a library file or one MOSS-SoundEffect \citep{moss2026} take, gated on a CLAP \citep{wu2022clap} score of at least 0.30.

\textbf{People.} People are models like any other: a close-up per person row, built by Pixal3D and painted by the finish route. SOMA's body \citep{saito2026soma}, read from the close-up by SAM 3D Body \citep{yang2026sam3dbody}, is fitted under it as its rig, and Kimodo's clips \citep{rempe2026kimodo} move it; a shell gate passes a look when 99 percent of its soft points lie within 5 cm of the body. Each person stands on a person spot of its scene's plan.

\textbf{Sim bodies.} Each character also gets a \textbf{sim body}, its physical twin on Newton, so people in an environment are simulated, not only animated. The minimal body is built straight from SOMA's 23 segments with a ball joint at each SOMA joint frame, driven by the character's SOMA segment rotations with no scaling and no per-person setup, its mass spread by its segment volumes, standing on a SCORE floor so its ground forces come from contact; muscles enter as a minimal first step. Built this way, the body follows SOMA's rotations to within 6.9e-9 m, and on Newton it matches plain MuJoCo to 3.4e-8 after 200 steps.

\textbf{Completion.} A scene is complete when its gate passes on its final assembly: every row made or dropped with a reason, nothing unmade, no resting fault, every layer and shown scene present. A fault the creator ships with is an accepted gap, written with who decided, when and why; the gate lists every gap by name and is never loosened. A hook stops an agent claiming a scene is done while its gate fails, since agents judging their own scenes agree with a measured geometry score only 45.8 percent of the time \citep{li2026lego}. A scene's films are made from scripts the creator locked, once it is complete.
