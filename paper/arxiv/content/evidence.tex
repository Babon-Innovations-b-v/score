\section{Evidence}\label{evidence}

Environment 1 is the worked example: 26 places, from a city square and a flat to a Moon base, a ship and Mars, derived from the game 2099. Its first run ended on 18 October; its remake from new designs has not run. Table 3 sorts the framework's parts into what these runs prove, what is built but has not run, and what is missing.

\textbf{The first run.} Each place was filmed once its completion gate passed or failed only on its accepted gaps: a 2 s title card, then one walk of 4 s (96 frames at 24 fps) on a path the planner keeps clear of walls, objects and people, with the place's own sound. The film gate measures each film (its size, a sound track that is not silent, no black or frozen stretch after the title card, its length and the clearance) and the runner accepts it; no person reviewed the films before the cut, and the coding agent that made the cut checked only its order, frames and sound. On 18 October the cut held all 27 films, 26 places and the ship's hull, 3 min 9 s (Figure 3). The final completion gate, run again on every place on 17 and 18 October, left all 26 places complete apart from accepted gaps (2 to 19 each) and none complete; many of those gaps were decided by the overseer, the coding-agent session that guards the run under the creator's rules, and wait for the creator's confirmation. The creator watched the video on 18 October and did not accept it, naming five faults: the ship looks poor, weakest where his own direction stopped; the films cut too fast, about 3.5 s a place; no people appear; the sound is only ambient, with no voices or event sounds; and moving things do not move convincingly. On 19 October the disk holding the store, both spend ledgers and the work folder was lost; the code, the cloud bucket and the library's entry records survived, and the store is rebuilt only by today's keys and gates, so most of the run's outputs must be made again.

\textbf{Layouts from the source game.} In the first run the framework's own layout, made from the plan and the concept, was proven only on the ship's 8 places and the 2 door kits. The other 18 places took their layouts from the source game: 507 entries in their data cite the game, 338 of them positions, and 7 kits are laid in the game's frame, with 6,432 piece positions. The check sees cited numbers only, so an uncited copy passes: the launch's accepted scene record still lit the rocket at the game's position. Each of the 18 places shipped this as an accepted gap of the completion gate's game check. The remake's plans are written from each place's own concept and setting instead.

\textbf{People.} Scored on what is drawn, no clip of any look is clean (Table 2). Takes in a wider A-pose crossed less at the bind and more in motion (10,610 visible crossings on the player's clips against 4,078), with no clip clean in either pose. People therefore shipped as accepted gaps in the first run: the characters layer lays the people whose bodies stand accepted, and the completion gate fails every missing one by name.

\textbf{The design step, 20 and 21 October.} For the remake the creator drew 95 concepts, EUR 14.28 together, and picked 18 of them. Their readings (41, one refused) cost EUR 2.56: the latest of each place holds 560 rows, 541 of them with metres on three sides, and 893 gaps where the reading and SAM 3 disagree or a row has no metres. The plan writer wrote all 18 plans without a refusal, EUR 0.13 to 0.74 and 62 to 325 s each, EUR 5.09 together, and the plan row accepted 17 of them (the base, an outside shell with no plan record of its own, has none). The environment plan was written twice, EUR 0.20 each. Eight draws, one for each of the ship's rooms, were drawn on a blockout; none is picked yet.

\textbf{The remake.} On 21 October the estimate for the batch of the 18 designed places stood at EUR 186.50 likely and 271.89 high for 456 models, with 45 gaps predicted from the hub's measured failures priced apart and not bought, and an ETA of at least 247 minutes with nine layers still untimed. The runner's own plan for every line priced 158 of its 231 paid rows from measured units, EUR 208.87 likely and 314.78 high; the other 73 rows (EUR 194.23 of estimates, among them the films, rough models, people takes, light passes and surface tiles) have no measured run and so no price. Two benchmark batches measured stages before it: six preview clips for EUR 0.96, five right and one effect blank and refused by its check, and eleven sound takes for EUR 0.05, two of them gaps by their likeness check. Nothing of the remake has run.
