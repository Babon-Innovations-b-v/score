\section{Evidence}\label{evidence}

Four environments are planned; environment 1's 28 places are judged once their layers stand accepted.

\textbf{Layouts from the source game.} The framework's own layout, made from the plan and the concept, is proven only on the ship's 8 places and the 2 door kits. The other 18 places still take their layouts from the source game: 507 entries in their data cite the game, 338 of them positions, and 7 kits are laid in the game's frame, with 6,432 piece positions. The module rooms' shells, laid from the game's shell numbers, are not in these counts. The check sees cited numbers only, so an uncited copy passes: the launch's accepted scene record still lights the rocket at the game's position. Each of the 18 places ships this as an accepted gap of the completion gate's game check (Table 3); deriving their layouts again from plan and concept is a rebuild not yet priced.

\textbf{People.} Scored on what is drawn, no clip of any look is clean (Table 2). Takes in a wider A-pose crossed less at the bind and more in motion (10,610 visible crossings on the player's clips against 4,078), with no clip clean in either pose. People therefore ship as accepted gaps: the characters layer lays the people whose bodies stand accepted, and the completion gate fails every missing one by name.

\textbf{The environment 1 video.} Each place is filmed once its completion gate passes or fails only on its accepted gaps: a 2 s title card, then one walk of 4 s (96 frames at 24 fps) on a path the planner keeps clear of walls, objects and people, with the place's own sound. The film gate measures each film (its size, a sound track that is not silent, no black or frozen stretch after the title card, its length and the clearance) and the runner accepts it; no person reviewed the films before the cut, and the coding agent that made the cut checked only its order, frames and sound. One cut opens with the creator's concept for every place, then takes the places in story order, the prologue, the Moon base and the Moon, the ship, then Mars: each place's concept, plan and pieces where it has them, then 3.5 s of its accepted film's walk, with the ship's hull after the hangar; it closes on the layer stack of one place and what environment 1 delivers for score\_lib. On 18 October it held all 27 films, 26 places and the hull, 3 min 9 s (Figure 3). Four of them, the greenhouse, the garage, the hangar and the hull, were filmed again that day: the ground outside a joined tube stood up to 2 m inside those rooms, because the scene writer (tools/usd/scene.py, write\_places) does not switch off a shown place's outside, and in the greenhouse it buried the crop bed; their film records now leave that ground out of the film's copy of the stage, and the fix in the scene writer is next month's. The airlock's film starts 6 s into its cycle, after its pump's fog: inside that fog a frame took from 47 s to 17 min on a card, and its first run, started in the fog, was stopped at the spend stop with its frames lost. Every filmed place was filmed after a completion gate that left it complete apart from accepted gaps. The final completion gate, run again on every place on 17 and 18 October, left all 26 places complete apart from accepted gaps (2 to 19 each) and none complete. The 2 door kits are not filmed; they are seen in the places they close.

\textbf{Agent tools.} With them an agent used about half the output and cost on two of three places; on the third it left more faults; one run each (Appendix E).
