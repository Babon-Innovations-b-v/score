\section{Evidence}\label{evidence}

We build four worlds with the same stages: the game world of 2099 (a prologue on Earth, a Moon base and a camp on Mars), a stylised underwater world, a simulation world for robots, and a third-person fantasy world. World 1 has 18 places, each its own line of the table, assembled and judged complete once, when every layer it needs stands accepted.

\textbf{Gap: Table 1, results per place and stage, and the film.} Per place: inventory rows, models generated and built in code, the layers present, faults caught before and after spending, time, euros from Scaleway's and Google's bills, and the creator's verdict; and a four-minute walk through world 1 from each place's own cameras with its sound. They wait on each place's one assembly and completion gate.

\end{minipage}}\end{center}

\textbf{With and without the agent tools.} On two of three places of world 1, an agent with the completion gate and its tools used about half the output and cost of one without them; on the third, the one without left fewer faults. Each arm ran once (Appendix E).
