\section{Evidence}\label{evidence}

Four worlds are planned; world 1's 28 places are judged once their layers stand accepted.

\textbf{Layouts from the source game.} The framework's own layout, made from the plan and the concept, is proven only on the ship's 8 places and the 2 door kits. The other 18 places still take their layouts from the source game: 507 entries in their data cite the game, 338 of them positions, and 7 kits are laid in the game's frame, with 6,432 piece positions. The module rooms' shells, laid from the game's shell numbers, are not in these counts. The check sees cited numbers only, so an uncited copy passes: the launch's accepted scene record still lights the rocket at the game's position. Each of the 18 places ships this as a named gap of the completion gate's game check (Table 3); deriving their layouts again from plan and concept is a rebuild not yet priced.

\textbf{People.} Scored on what is drawn, no clip of any look is clean (Table 2). Takes in a wider A-pose crossed less at the bind and more in motion (10,610 visible crossings on the player's clips against 4,078), with no clip clean in either pose. People therefore ship as named gaps: the characters layer lays the people whose bodies stand accepted, and the completion gate fails every missing one by name.
