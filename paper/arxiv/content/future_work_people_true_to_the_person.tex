\section{Future work: people true to the person}\label{future-work-people-true-to-the-person}

The minimal sim body is a start. This section states goals, not results.

\textbf{Bodies true to the person.} The sim body is to become fully musculoskeletal and true to the actual person, older people and a curved spine included, not a template body resized: joint centres from bone landmarks on its SOMA mesh, then mass, muscles, joint ranges, spine and bone shape, each input SOMA lacks marked as assumed with a range. A full musculoskeletal body with 416 muscles already runs on Newton but still drifts 1.5 cm from plain MuJoCo after 300 steps, so Newton is to be validated for bodies before more is built on it. Its joint angles, then its joint moments and ground forces, are to be held to OpenCap's LabValidation data.

\textbf{Scenes from motion.} Motion is to come from the SOMA ecosystem: GEM-X \citep{gemx} from video, Kimodo from sentences, and sequences joined from datasets of both. The terrain a motion implies becomes a SCORE scene and a sim body follows the motion on it, so the scene keeps the motion with no video and nothing that identifies a person.

\textbf{Brains.} A \textbf{brain} is a trained model that drives a sim body's muscles so it moves on its own, including over structures it never saw. Trained on sim-body data and placed in SCORE's invented worlds, under their own gravity and ground, it is to adapt to what no recording holds. Specialised models, for example for clinical groups, would be trained outside SCORE on data that never enters it.

\textbf{The data loop.} Sim bodies moving in synthesized scenes make data; that data trains better and more specialised brains; a brain drives a sim body in new scenes, which makes more data.
