\section{Future work: people true to the person}\label{future-work-people-true-to-the-person}

The minimal sim body is a start, and what follows are goals rather than results.

\textbf{Bodies true to the person.} The sim body is to become fully musculoskeletal and true to the person, older people and a curved spine included, instead of a resized template. Joint centres are to come from bone landmarks on its SOMA mesh, then mass, muscles, joint ranges, spine and bone shape, each input SOMA lacks marked as assumed with a range. A full musculoskeletal body with 416 muscles already runs on Newton but still drifts 1.5 cm from plain MuJoCo after 300 steps, so Newton is to be validated for bodies first. Its joint angles, then its joint moments and ground forces, are to be held to OpenCap's LabValidation data.

\textbf{Scenes from motion.} Motion is to come from the SOMA ecosystem, with GEM-X \citep{gemx} reading it from video, Kimodo making it from sentences, and sequences joined from datasets of both. The terrain a motion implies becomes a SCORE scene where a sim body follows it, so the scene keeps the motion without video or anything identifying a person.

\textbf{Brains.} A brain is a trained model that drives a sim body's muscles so it moves on its own, including over structures it never saw. Trained on sim-body data and placed in SCORE's invented worlds under their own gravity and ground, it is to adapt to what no recording holds. Specialised models, for example for clinical groups, would be trained outside SCORE on data that never enters it.

\textbf{The data loop.} Sim bodies moving in synthesised scenes make data that trains better and more specialised brains, and a brain drives a sim body in new scenes, which makes more data.
