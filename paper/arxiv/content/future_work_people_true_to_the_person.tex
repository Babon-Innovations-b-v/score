\section{Future work: people true to the person}\label{future-work-people-true-to-the-person}

The minimal sim body is a starting point, and what follows describes goals rather than results.

\textbf{Bodies true to the person.} The sim body is to become a full musculoskeletal model that is specific to the individual, older people and people with a curved spine included, rather than a scaled generic template. Joint centres are to be derived from bony landmarks on the SOMA mesh, followed by segment masses, muscles, joint ranges of motion, the spine and bone geometry, with each input that SOMA does not provide marked as assumed and given a plausible range. A full-body musculoskeletal model with 416 muscles, MyoSuite's MyoFullBody \citep{caggiano2022myosuite}, whose arm and hand derive from the MoBL-ARMS model \citep{holzbaur2005upper, saul2015benchmarking}, already runs in Newton but still drifts 1.5 cm from plain MuJoCo after 300 steps, so Newton is first to be validated for such bodies. The body's joint angles, and then its net joint moments and ground reaction forces, are to be validated against OpenCap's LabValidation data.

\textbf{Scenes from motion.} Motion is to come from the SOMA ecosystem, either estimated from video with GEM-X \citep{gemx} or generated from text and constraints with Kimodo \citep{rempe2026kimodo}, in practically unlimited quantity. The terrain a motion implies becomes a SCORE scene, so the movement is kept in its setting without the source video or any information that identifies a person. The sim body then follows each movement as a prescribed trajectory on the scene's ground, and the simulation solves for the ground reaction forces from contact, the net joint moments, and the activations and forces of a 416-muscle model that reproduce it. Any force and moment that neither the ground contact nor the muscles can supply remains as the residual at the pelvis. It is recorded for every frame and never applied to the body, so it quantifies how dynamically consistent the movement is, and only movements with a small residual are kept. Biomechanics reduces and reports the same residual to assess recorded motion \citep{delp2007opensim, werling2023addbiomechanics}, and PhysibleMDM \citep{stolzle2026physiblemdm} minimises an inverse-dynamics residual to make generated motion feasible for a torque-driven robot; we apply the criterion to generated motion in a muscle-driven body.

\textbf{Layered training sets.} The same simulations yield layered training sets. Each sample pairs a rendered image of a clothed person in a complete scene with physics-derived ground truth for every layer beneath it, namely the SOMA body and its joint angles, the ground reaction force under each foot from simulated contact, the muscle activations and forces, the residual, and the depth, segmentation and layout of the scene. Their main purpose is to train estimators that infer muscle activations, joint moments and ground reaction forces directly from monocular video, quickly enough for routine clinical use. Recorded data cannot provide this supervision at scale, because muscle forces cannot be measured non-invasively and force plates and electromyography confine measurement to a laboratory, whereas the simulation can generate the bodies, movements and settings an estimator has to cover, including those of a specific population, so that domain-specific estimators can be trained for it. BioHuman10M \citep{huo2026biohuman} already pairs video, part of it rendered, with the activations of a 112-muscle model and with ground reaction forces predicted by a learned model from recorded motion. Our sets would differ in scenes generated for the purpose under varied lighting, terrain and clothing, in ground reaction forces from contact with each scene's own ground, in a 416-muscle model and in a residual for every sample, and their value depends on whether estimators trained on them transfer to real force-plate and electromyography recordings.

\textbf{Brains.} A brain is a trained controller that actuates a sim body's muscles, and its role is to extend the training data beyond what a motion generator produces. Trained on the retained movements together with the muscle forces that produce them, it is placed in SCORE's synthesised worlds under their own gravity and terrain, where it is to adapt to structures no recording contains, and each run it drives adds movement to the training sets. Muscle-driven controllers such as KINESIS \citep{simos2025kinesis} and MuscleMimic \citep{li2026musclemimic} are trained on motion capture, as are torque-driven ones such as PHC \citep{luo2023phc}. To our knowledge, no muscle-driven controller has yet been trained on generated motion selected by its residual, and the claim holds only once such a controller is compared with one trained on motion capture and with one trained on the same generated motion unfiltered.

\textbf{The data loop.} The amount of data the loop produces scales with the compute put behind it, since every scene, movement and run adds samples. This is why SCORE is open, so that others can apply their own compute to the same framework and share the resulting datasets. Estimators specialised for clinical use would be trained outside SCORE, on data that never enters it.
