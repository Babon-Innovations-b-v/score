\section{Future work: people true to the person}\label{future-work-people-true-to-the-person}

The minimal sim body is a start, and what follows are goals rather than results.

\textbf{Bodies true to the person.} The sim body is to become fully musculoskeletal and true to the person, older people and a curved spine included, instead of a resized template. Joint centres are to come from bone landmarks on its SOMA mesh, then mass, muscles, joint ranges, spine and bone shape, each input SOMA lacks marked as assumed with a range. A full musculoskeletal body with 416 muscles already runs on Newton but still drifts 1.5 cm from plain MuJoCo after 300 steps, so Newton is to be validated for bodies first. Its joint angles, then its joint moments and ground forces, are to be held to OpenCap's LabValidation data.

\textbf{Scenes from motion.} Motion is to come from the SOMA ecosystem, with GEM-X \citep{gemx} reading it from video, Kimodo \citep{rempe2026kimodo} making it from sentences without limit, and sequences joined from datasets of both. The terrain a motion implies becomes a SCORE scene, so the scene keeps the motion without video or anything identifying a person. A sim body follows each movement exactly on its scene's ground, and physics computes the ground contact, the joint torques and the forces of the 416 muscles the movement needs. What neither the ground nor the muscles can supply is the residual, recorded for every frame and never applied, so it measures how physically possible the movement is, and only movement with a small residual is kept. Biomechanics already reduces and reports this residual as a measure of the quality of recorded motion \citep{delp2007opensim, werling2023addbiomechanics}, and PhysibleMDM \citep{stolzle2026physiblemdm} lowers an inverse-dynamics residual to make generated motion feasible for a torque-driven robot. We apply the same check to generated motion played through a muscle-driven body.

\textbf{Layered training sets.} The same runs can yield layered training sets. Each sample is a rendered picture of a clothed person in a full scene, paired with exact values for every layer beneath it, from the SOMA body and joint angles, the ground force under each foot from simulated contact and the muscle activations and forces to the residual, depth, segmentation and the scene's layout. Their main use is to train fast models that read muscle activity, joint moments and ground forces from ordinary video. No recorded dataset can teach this at scale, since muscle forces cannot be measured directly in people and force plates and muscle sensors confine recording to a laboratory, whereas the simulation can generate the bodies, movements and settings a model must cover, including those of a particular group, so that a model can be specialised to it. BioHuman10M \citep{huo2026biohuman} already pairs video, rendered clips among it, with the activations of a 112-muscle body and with ground forces estimated by a learned model from recorded motion. Our sets would differ in scenes made for the purpose in any light, ground and clothing, in forces from contact with each scene's own ground, in a 416-muscle body and in a residual for every sample, and their worth rests on a model trained on them carrying over to real force-plate and muscle recordings.

\textbf{Brains.} A brain is a trained model that drives a sim body's muscles so it moves on its own, and its role here is to widen the data beyond what a motion generator offers. Trained on the kept movements together with the muscle forces that produce them, it is placed in SCORE's synthesised worlds under their own gravity and ground, where it is to adapt to structures no recording holds, and every run it drives adds movement for the training sets. Muscle-driven controllers such as KINESIS \citep{simos2025kinesis} and MuscleMimic \citep{li2026musclemimic} learn from motion capture, as torque-driven ones such as PHC \citep{luo2023phc} do. To our knowledge, no muscle-driven controller has yet been trained on generated motion filtered by its residual, and the claim holds only once such a brain is compared with one trained on motion capture and with one trained on the same generated motion unfiltered.

\textbf{The data loop.} Models specialised for clinical use would be trained outside SCORE on data that never enters it. The value of the loop grows with the compute put behind it, since every scene, movement and run adds data, which is why SCORE is open, so that others can put their own compute behind the same framework and share the sets they make back.
