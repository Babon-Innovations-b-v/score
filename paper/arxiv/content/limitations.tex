\section{Limitations}\label{limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{One creator, one world so far, and that world not yet accepted.} Every verdict on the results is one creator's, and only the first world has been built. Most of its places do not yet pass their own completion gate (Appendix B), and the creator has accepted one place, the hub.
\item
  \textbf{Code builders do not scale well.} They give clean objects, but a coding agent has to write one for every kind of object.
\item
  \textbf{Places come out sparser than their concepts.} The concept-density check catches this but does not fix it.
\item
  \textbf{Two stages depend on closed, paid models.} Nano Banana Pro draws the concepts and every close-up that the open model's pictures fail; on the last 17 close-ups of world 1, none of the open model's pictures passed the shape check (Appendix E). The surface stage uses two of Google's models: Gemini 3.1 Pro lists each model's finishes on its close-up and names their library surfaces, and Nano Banana Pro draws the finish map that SegviGen lays onto the shape. A public framework thus depends on closed services for two of its stages. Their daily limits set the pace of world 1, and they can change their price or be retired, which limits reproducibility. An open-model fallback for both stages is future work.
\item
  \textbf{Parts do not follow finishes, and shadow reads as paint.} PartCrafter's parts follow shape, not paint, so a finish without its own part, such as a gold foil patch on a white hull, is lost when the part is painted with one surface, and the judge that names each part's surface reads shadow on the close-up as dark paint. A split seeded by the close-up's regions (GeoSAM2 \citep{deng2025geosam2}) owns more finishes in a comparison on 22 models, but is not yet part of the route.
\item
  \textbf{Objects are physical but not interactive.} Seats, terminals and other usable objects do not work in the output.
\item
  \textbf{Characters' clothes are rejected, and animals' motion is rough.} The creator rejected the Newton drapes that every crew and cast body wears, as bloated at the chest and sleeves, and they fail a silhouette check against Warp's drapes; of the open routes tried since, Newton with settings per look passes the work suits but no space suit, and Pixal3D's mesh with SOMA-X's skeleton fitted inside it fails the check that the clothes stay tucked in. The animals' clips are made in code: the dog's paws twist, its sit reads as a crouch and its feet slide by up to 5.4 cm.
\end{itemize}
