\section{Limitations}\label{limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{One creator, one world so far, and that world not yet accepted.} Every verdict on the results is one creator's, and only the first world has been built. Most of its places do not yet pass their own completion gate: on 10 October, under that day's gate (reach counted, the first placeholder check), 3 of 18 passed, and the creator has accepted one place, the hub.
\item
  \textbf{Code builders do not scale well.} They give clean objects, but a coding agent has to write one for every kind of object.
\item
  \textbf{Places come out sparser than their concepts.} The concept-density check catches this but does not fix it.
\item
  \textbf{One stage still depends on a closed, paid picture model.} Nano Banana Pro, a closed and paid service, draws the concepts and every close-up that the open model's pictures fail. On the last 17 close-ups of world 1, none of the open model's pictures passed the shape check, so the closed model drew every close-up that was accepted. Its daily limits set the pace of world 1, and it can change or be retired, which limits reproducibility and leaves a public framework depending on a closed service for one stage. A cheaper model of the same family did not replace it (Appendix E).
\item
  \textbf{Parts do not follow finishes, and shadow reads as paint.} PartCrafter's parts follow shape, not paint, so a finish that does not have its own part, such as a gold foil patch on a white hull or a brown seat on a grey frame, is still lost when the part is painted with one surface, and the judge that names each part's surface reads a region the close-up shows in shadow as dark paint. On world 1 the painting is therefore not yet consistent beyond the hub; a split seeded by the close-up's regions (GeoSAM2 \citep{deng2025geosam2}) owns more finishes in a comparison on 22 models, but is not yet part of the route or shown on a repainted model.
\item
  \textbf{Objects are physical but not interactive.} Seats, terminals and other usable objects do not work in the output.
\item
  \textbf{Characters' clothes are rejected, and animals' motion is rough.} The creator rejected the Newton drapes that every crew and cast body wears, as bloated at the chest and sleeves, and they fail the silhouette check against Warp's drapes; of the open routes tried since, Newton with settings per look passes the work suits but no space suit. The drapes are a trial until one route passes that check on every look and then the creator's eye. A second way to make a person, Pixal3D's mesh with SOMA-X's skeleton fitted inside it, is also a trial: its first person still fails the check that the clothes stay tucked in. The animals' clips are made in code: the dog's paws twist, its sit reads as a crouch and its feet slide by up to 5.4 cm.
\end{itemize}
