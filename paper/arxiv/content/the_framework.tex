Figure 1 shows the stages on one place, from the creator's concept to the assembled room. Every stage's output is kept, so the creator can trace a weak result to its stage and rerun or fix that stage alone. Appendix C lists every model, its licence, and what was automatic or done by hand.

\textbf{Inventory.} A coding agent cuts the concept into tiles and lists every visible element, and each element becomes a row, with its kind, anchor, size and count, or is dropped with a written reason. Nothing is made or placed that is not a row.

\textbf{Close-ups.} Every inventory row gets a close-up, a picture of the object alone, drawn from its crop of the concept by an open model, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 \citep{qwenimageedit}; only a close-up that fails the shape check (Section 3.1) is redrawn by a closed one, Nano Banana Pro \citep{nanobananapro}.

\textbf{Models.} Each object is built by a code builder that a coding agent writes or generated from its close-up by Pixal3D \citep{li2026pixal3d}, as the parts check decides (Section 3.2). Every real-world object becomes one model: a console becomes a desk carrying separate monitors and keyboards. Models give shape only (Section 3.3). Outdoor ground follows a plan of flat areas and paths, with landforms from a picture drawn over it.

\textbf{The canonical scene.} Every place is one OpenUSD stage \citep{openusd} in which each object carries its kind, its transform in metres, its collision and the library surface of each part, together with the room's shell, the ground, the backdrop, the lights and the cameras the place is judged from. The generated base layer lies under an edit layer that belongs to the creator, so an edit survives when the base is generated again (Figure 2).

\textbf{The other layers.} The same stage holds the rest of what makes a place complete, each in a layer of its own: people, as skinned characters that a chain of open models makes from a picture or a few words; sound, generated by MOSS-SoundEffect \citep{moss2026} and tied to surfaces, rooms and objects; motion, as time samples; particle effects; and shaders, baked into pictures any USD reader can draw. Every stage that loads a model runs as a batch on rented cards that are deleted afterwards, or as a job on a Kubernetes cluster.
