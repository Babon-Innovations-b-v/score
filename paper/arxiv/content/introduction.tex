\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Interactive 3D worlds are the raw material of games, film and embodied AI \citep{yang2024holodeck}, and building one is expensive: every object has to be modelled, surfaced, placed, given collision, lit and given sound in one style, and the world has to stay editable. Generative models promise to take over much of this, but in GDC's 2026 survey 52 percent of game workers thought generative AI was hurting the industry \citep{gdc2026survey}; a tool for building worlds has to leave the result in the creator's hands.

World models generate a world as frames \citep{bruce2024genie, genie3} that no engine can open. 3D world generators \citep{wang2025worldgen, hyworld2} produce geometry, but mostly as one fused asset, from which separate objects are recovered after the fact \citep{hu2026worldact, niu2026worldsculpt}. Both work in one shot, so a creator cannot find or change the step behind a fault. Procedural systems \citep{raistrick2023infinigen, raistrick2024indoors} and coding agents that write scenes as programs \citep{hu2024scenecraft, li2026rcwm, guo2026worldclaw, yin2026autoue} keep the world explicit, but rebuild a scene that already exists or draw their objects from a fixed library, and such agents judge their own geometry unreliably \citep{li2026lego}. Open vision foundation models \citep{oquab2023dinov2} now read shapes \citep{li2026pixal3d}, masks \citep{carion2025sam3}, depth \citep{wang2025moge2} and parts \citep{lin2025partcrafter} out of pictures, and a coding agent can connect them as tools; none of them provides is one style across objects that different models made, which the field names as an open problem \citep{wu2026production, yang2026flowscene}.

Studios build worlds in stages, each with an output a person can check. SCORE keeps these stages and automates large parts of them with the models above, procedural tools and coding agents, while the creator chooses and reviews. Its worlds are \textbf{complete}: every object, surface, light and sound exists wherever the player looks. They are \textbf{owned}: every model in the framework allows commercial use of its output, so the creator stays the author, holding the full commercial rights. They are \textbf{responsive}: every object has real collision, mass and material. And they can be any kind of \textbf{environment}. To our knowledge, no other openly available world generation work joins vision foundation models into engine-ready scenes that are built from reference pictures, steered by a person's choices at each step, and made only with models that allow commercial use of their output.

This paper makes three contributions:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{The SCORE framework:} staged, retraceable generation from a creator's choices to one canonical scene: a generated base layer under the creator's edit layers (Section 2).
\item
  \textbf{Mechanisms that keep worlds coherent and faults cheap:} checks before every paid stage, a parts check, a shared surface library that keeps shape and surface apart, and review tools (Section 3).
\item
  \textbf{Evidence from four worlds of different kinds}, and a shared benchmark (Section 4).
\end{itemize}
