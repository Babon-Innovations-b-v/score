\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Interactive 3D environments are the raw material of games, film and embodied AI \citep{yang2024holodeck}, and building one is expensive: every object is modelled, surfaced, placed, given collision, lit and given sound in one style, and the environment stays editable. Generative models promise to take over much of this, but in GDC's 2026 survey 52 percent of game workers thought generative AI was hurting the industry \citep{gdc2026survey}; a tool for building environments has to leave the result in the creator's hands.

World models generate an environment as frames \citep{bruce2024genie, genie3} that no engine can open. 3D environment generators \citep{wang2025worldgen, hyworld2} produce geometry, but mostly as one fused asset, from which separate objects are recovered after the fact \citep{hu2026worldact, niu2026worldsculpt}. Both work in one shot, so a creator cannot find or change the step behind a fault. Procedural systems \citep{raistrick2023infinigen, raistrick2024indoors} and coding agents that write scenes as programs \citep{hu2024scenecraft, li2026rcwm, guo2026worldclaw, yin2026autoue} keep the environment explicit, but rebuild a scene that already exists or draw their objects from a fixed library, and such agents judge their own geometry unreliably \citep{li2026lego}. Open vision foundation models \citep{oquab2023dinov2} now read shapes \citep{li2026pixal3d}, masks \citep{carion2025sam3}, depth \citep{wang2025moge2} and parts \citep{lin2025partcrafter} out of pictures, and a coding agent can connect them as tools; none of them provides one style across objects that different models made, which the field names as an open problem \citep{wu2026production, yang2026flowscene}.

Recent agents write Blender programs and check them against a target: from one image, in stages of geometry, materials, composition and light \citep{he2026thinking}; from text or video, as worlds for physical reasoning \citep{wang2026codeasworlds}; and with motion added \citep{zhang2026code2worlds, liu2026simworlds}. Benchmarks find that their procedural models often float or come apart \citep{gao2026codebench3d}, and that on dynamic scenes rebuilt from video the best model scores 0.91 on static scenes but 0.67 on dynamic ones; mesh quality does not track fidelity, and VLM judges agree with people in 89.3 percent of comparisons but near chance between close models \citep{shen2026codebench4d}. SCORE builds static environments in explicit, gated stages and treats motion as its own checked layer, which this gap between static and dynamic scenes supports.

Studios build environments in stages, each with an output a person can check; SCORE keeps these stages and automates them. Its environments are \textbf{complete}: every object, surface, light and sound exists wherever the player looks. They are \textbf{owned}: every model in the framework allows commercial use of its output, so the creator stays the author, holding the full commercial rights. They are \textbf{responsive}: every object has real collision, mass and material. And they can be any kind of \textbf{environment}. To our knowledge, no other openly available environment generation work joins vision foundation models into engine-ready scenes that are built from reference pictures, steered by a person's choices at each step, and made only with models that allow commercial use of their output.

We contribute \textbf{the SCORE framework}, stages as nodes of one runner that keys, judges and accepts every output over one canonical scene (Section 2); \textbf{measured gates} for every layer of a place and a completion gate over them (Section 3); and \textbf{evidence from a first environment}, with its spend per stage (Section 4).
