\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Simulated 3D environments serve games, film, robot learning and embodied AI \citep{yang2024holodeck}. They share a structure of separate objects with collision, mass and joints, ground with a gravity, light and sound that come from somewhere, and scenes that can be opened, changed and simulated again. Building one by hand is expensive, and a generator that takes the work over must leave the result in its maker's hands, since in GDC's 2026 survey 52 percent of game workers thought generative AI was hurting the industry \citep{gdc2026survey}.

World models generate an environment as frames \citep{bruce2024genie, genie3} that no simulator can step. 3D environment generators \citep{wang2025worldgen, hyworld2} produce geometry, mostly as one fused asset from which separate objects are recovered afterwards \citep{hu2026worldact, niu2026worldsculpt}. Both work in one shot, so a fault cannot be traced to its step. Procedural systems \citep{raistrick2023infinigen, raistrick2024indoors} and coding agents that write scenes as programs \citep{hu2024scenecraft, li2026rcwm, guo2026worldclaw, yin2026autoue, he2026thinking, wang2026codeasworlds} keep the environment explicit but rebuild a scene that already exists or draw from a fixed library. Their models often float or come apart \citep{gao2026codebench3d}, such agents judge their own geometry unreliably \citep{li2026lego}, and on scenes rebuilt from video the best model scores 0.91 on static scenes but 0.67 on dynamic ones \citep{shen2026codebench4d}. Open vision foundation models \citep{oquab2023dinov2} now read shapes \citep{li2026pixal3d}, masks \citep{carion2025sam3} and depth \citep{wang2025moge2} from pictures, and a coding agent can connect them as tools, yet none gives one style across objects that different models made \citep{wu2026production, yang2026flowscene}, and none gives physics.

The pieces for the physics exist. OpenUSD \citep{openusd}, the scene description Pixar made for its films, keeps each department's work in its own layer file and assembles scenes from smaller ones by reference, and its physics schema states bodies, colliders, mass and joints in the same stage. MuJoCo \citep{todorov2012mujoco} is a widely used solver for articulated bodies with contact, and Newton \citep{newton}, built on Warp \citep{warp}, runs MuJoCo's solver on the GPU \citep{mujocowarp} and reads OpenUSD directly. What is missing is a route from what a creator wants to a complete environment in this form.

SCORE is that framework. Its environments are complete, since every object, surface, light and sound a scene shows exists wherever the viewer looks. They are owned, because every model in the framework allows commercial use of its output, so the creator holds the full rights. They are responsive, because every object has its collider, mass and material and what moves has its joints, so a simulator steps the environment as written. They are editable, since each scene is a stack of layers made by keyed steps and a change reruns only what it touches. An environment can be a house on a plot of ground or a system of worlds that move one another.

We contribute the SCORE framework (Section 2), which turns a creator's locked design into one OpenUSD environment that is physically simulable and opens unchanged in a game engine, a robot simulator or a film renderer. Its method (Section 3) builds a scene top down and layer by layer, each stage behind measured gates. The evidence plan (Section 4) comprises four demonstrations on a first environment, left as gaps until its batch has run. Section 5 sets out where the framework goes next, toward simulated people true to the person.
