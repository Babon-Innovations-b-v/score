\section{Related work}\label{related-work}

\textbf{World models and world generators.} Genie learns an interactive environment from video \citep{bruce2024genie}; WorldGen generates traversable worlds from text \citep{wang2025worldgen}; WorldAct and WorldSculpt recover separate objects from generated worlds or video \citep{hu2026worldact, niu2026worldsculpt}. They generate the world itself; SCORE uses a world generator at most as an optional reference step and ships only the scenes its stages build.

\textbf{Agents that build scenes.} Holodeck solves language-model layout constraints over retrieved objects \citep{yang2024holodeck}; SceneCraft and recursive code world models write scenes as programs \citep{hu2024scenecraft, li2026rcwm}; WorldClaw and AutoUE assemble open worlds and game code with agents \citep{guo2026worldclaw, yin2026autoue}. LEGO-Anything finds that coding agents start scenes weakly, regress while editing and judge geometry unreliably, and answers with tools rather than training \citep{li2026lego}. SCORE applies that stance to worlds that do not exist yet, built to a person's picks rather than rebuilt from a photograph.

\textbf{Procedural generation, parts and style.} Infinigen and Infinigen Indoors generate worlds and rooms from rules, with materials as their own generators \citep{raistrick2023infinigen, raistrick2024indoors}; ProcFunc gives them an interface language models write well \citep{raistrick2026procfunc}. PartCrafter and Point2Part split shapes into parts \citep{lin2025partcrafter, tsui2026point2part}. Style across generated assets remains open \citep{wu2026production, yang2026flowscene}; Hunyuan3D Studio fixes it at the picture \citep{lei2025hunyuanstudio}. SCORE takes surface out of the generated models entirely.
