\begin{abstract}
Games, film, robotics and embodied AI need 3D environments in which
every object is a physical body of its own, that remain editable, and
that belong to whoever made them. SCORE is an open framework that builds
such environments as OpenUSD scenes from a creator's locked concept
picture. Its first method derived each scene from that picture through a
chain of model readings, plans and generated shells; on 26 places it
reached a mean of 2.2 out of 10 against its concepts and no place read
as its concept. We replace that chain with kit placement: a coding agent
lays pieces from an owned asset library in Blender, renders the
concept's own view, compares the render with the concept and corrects,
while a physics-readiness check in MuJoCo refuses anything that floats,
interpenetrates, tips, blocks a walkway or leaves a door outside its
opening. Missing props are made with the open TRELLIS.2 model and graded
to one palette per place, real terrain is read from public-domain HiRISE
elevation data, and walkers follow routes found on the checked floor,
generated as joined Kimodo segments and given a musculoskeletal sim
body. On four places the method reaches 4 to 7 out of 10 against the
concepts by its own critic, with no physics fault in any place, foot
skate under 3 cm and route error under 7 cm for 12 of 13 walkers; every
walker's sim body still fails the residual check at its peaks. Code:
https://github.com/Babon-Innovations-b-v/score (MIT).
\end{abstract}
